\textbf{Definition:} A series is known to be telescopic if some of the first few terms and final terms of a partial sum are left after the cancellation of most of the terms in the middle.

{Formally we let $S_n$ be defined as $\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n=a_1+a_2+a_3+\dots+a_n$ such that all terms are positive and permissible, $\forall n \in \mathbb{N}$.}

{We want to show that the telescoping series converges. Consequently, we want to prove that when $n$ approaches $+ \infty$, $S_n=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}$ gives a constant, say $S \in \mathbb{R}$. Hence, we are interested in $\lim\limits_{n \to +\infty} S_n=S$.}

\vspace{0.5cm} % Adjust the space above the line
\hrule % Or use \rule{\textwidth}{0.4pt}
\vspace{0.5cm} % Adjust the space below the line

\textbf{Proof.}

{Strategically, we let $S_n=a_n-a_{n+1}$. It follows that}
$$\lim_{n \to +\infty} S_n=\lim_{n \to +\infty} [a_n - a_{n+1}]=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} [a_n-a_{n+1}]$$

{By the distributive and linear property of summation, it becomes}

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} [a_n-a_{n+1}]=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n - \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_{n+1}$$

{Express in partial sums}
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n=a_1+a_2+a_3 + \dots+a_n$$

$$-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_{n+1} =-(a_2+a_3+a_4+\dots +a_{n+1})$$

By combining the two partial sums, we get:
\begin{align*}
    S_n &= (a_1 + a_2 + a_3 + \dots + a_n) - (a_2 + a_3 + a_4 + \dots + a_{n+1}) \\
    S_n &= a_1 - a_{n+1}
\end{align*}

{Taking the limit of the resulting expression}
\begin{align*}
    \lim_{n \to +\infty} S_n &= \lim_{n \to +\infty} [a_1 - a_{n+1}] \\
    &= \lim_{n \to +\infty} a_1 - \lim_{n \to +\infty} a_{n+1} \\
    &= a_1 - \lim_{n \to +\infty} a_{n+1}
\end{align*}

{If $\lim_{n \to +\infty} a_{n+1}$ exists, then $\lim\limits_{n \to +\infty} S_n$ also exists because $\lim\limits_{n \to +\infty} a_1=a_1$. Therefore, the telescoping series $S_n$ converges. \hfill $\square$}
